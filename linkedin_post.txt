Most retail forecasting systems are secretly just photocopiers.

They copy last year's seasonal pattern, adjust slightly for recent trend, and call it a forecast. Walmart ran one for years.

The problem: their model had no idea Thanksgiving moves. Or that one neighbourhood in New Orleans buys 3× the normal volume of a specific squash the week before the holiday while everywhere else stays flat. Same forecast run. Shortage in Louisiana. Waste everywhere else.

The fix wasn't a fancier algorithm. It was better features.

In 2018, Walmart rebuilt their "Smart Forecasting" platform on Gradient Boosting Machines — with explicit flags for shifting holidays, payroll and SNAP schedule signals, and store-level lag features that let the model learn *corrections on top of* what the old system already knew.

I reproduced the full pipeline on the public Kaggle Walmart dataset (45 stores, 81 departments, 143 weeks). Results on the held-out test period:

→ Seasonal Naive WAPE:  10.84%
→ LightGBM GBM WAPE:    8.27%
→ Improvement:          +2.57 pp  (23.7% relative reduction)
→ RMSE drop:            3,623  →  2,769  (−23.5%)

Scaled to Walmart's $611B revenue, McKinsey's supply chain benchmark puts that at roughly $785M in working-capital release — from one modelling upgrade.

The thing that surprised me most: the top feature by a wide margin was lag_52w — literally last year's same week. The GBM didn't discard it. It used it as a baseline and learned where to correct it. That's the whole story in one feature importance chart.
